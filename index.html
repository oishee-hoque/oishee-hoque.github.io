I'm a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the University of Virginia and a recipient of the UVA SEAS Endowed Fellowship 2025, advised by Dr. Madhav Marathe at the Biocomplexity Institute, University of Virginia. I build multimodal and vision–language models for detection, segmentation and scene understanding.

I'm currently an Applied Scientist Intern at Amazon Science, building multimodal AI for retail smart glasses: real-time on-device vision, cloud foundation models, and the hands-free AR and voice workflows on top. Before joining Amazon I spent a summer as a Graduate Research Intern in the GeoAI group at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, and before the PhD I worked as a software engineer at Enosis Solutions.

My PhD research is in computer vision for Earth observation: knowledge-guided and multimodal learning, vision–language models, and foundation-model adaptation for remote sensing, applied to agricultural and environmental monitoring. My PhD work runs along three threads:

Knowledge-guided Multimodal Learning & Vision

Segmentation and detection architectures that take domain structure as input instead of relearning it: crop and land-use priors through cross-attention, graph constraints on canal networks, and geometric priors on infrastructure.

  • KIIM IJCAI ’25
  • IGraSS IJCAI ’25
  • PRISM-CAFO WACV ’26

ML-ready datasets and benchmarks

Scalable pipelines that turn raw satellite and UAV archives into annotated detection and segmentation benchmarks: multimodal inputs, metadata, held-out sites and task-specific metrics.

  • IrrMap KDD ’25
  • IRRISIGHT NeurIPS ’25
  • CAFOSat CVPRW ’26
  • CWDBench WACV ’27

Foundation models and agentic geospatial systems

Adapting SAM-, DINO- and Swin-based models and vision–language models to remote-sensing detection and segmentation, and composing LLM agents with deterministic geospatial tools for operational decision support.

  • PRISM-CAFO WACV ’26
  • ARFA OASIS @ SIGSPATIAL ’26
  • CWDBench WACV ’27
Core skills
