About

I'm a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the University of Virginia and a recipient of the UVA SEAS Endowed Fellowship, advised by Madhav Marathe at the Biocomplexity Institute. My PhD research is in computer vision for Earth observation: knowledge-guided and multimodal learning, vision–language models, and foundation-model adaptation for remote sensing, applied to agricultural and environmental monitoring. I design models that fold domain priors into segmentation and detection, and I build the large-scale, ML-ready geospatial datasets that make those models trainable and testable.

I'm currently an Applied Scientist Intern on Amazon's GRAISE team in Seattle, building multimodal AI for retail smart glasses: real-time on-device vision, cloud foundation models, and the hands-free AR and voice workflows on top. Before joining Amazon I spent a summer as a Graduate Research Intern in Oak Ridge National Laboratory's GeoAI group, and before the PhD I worked as a software engineer at Enosis Solutions. Work from my PhD and internships has appeared at NeurIPS, KDD, IJCAI, WACV and CVPR workshops.

Research

Knowledge-guided multimodal models

Segmentation and detection models that take domain structure as input instead of relearning it: crop and land-use priors through cross-attention, graph constraints on canal networks, and geometric priors on infrastructure.

KIIM IGraSS PRISM-CAFO

ML-ready datasets and benchmarks

Scalable pipelines that turn raw satellite and UAV archives into annotated, multimodal benchmarks with metadata, held-out sites and task-specific metrics.

IrrMap IRRISIGHT CAFOSat CWDBench

Foundation models and agentic geospatial systems

Adapting SAM-, DINO- and Swin-based models and vision–language models to remote sensing, and composing LLM agents with deterministic geospatial tools for operational decision support.
